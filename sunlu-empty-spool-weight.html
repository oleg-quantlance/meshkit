How Much Does an Empty Sunlu Spool Weigh?

On file: 140–195 g depending on the spool type. Black plastic spools on standard lines; lighter cardboard 'eco' spools on newer stock. The number you need is the tare — the weight of the bare spool with no filament — because the only practical way to know how much filament is left on a partly used spool is to put it on a kitchen scale and subtract the tare. Manufacturers rarely print it on the label, and it changes when a brand switches from plastic to cardboard cores, so the table above is built from real measurements rather than spec sheets.

Every row shows how many measurements it rests on and how many came from the community. A single measurement is a reasonable estimate; three or more from different batches is a reliable one. If your own empty spool reads a few grams off, that is normal production variance — cardboard spools also absorb moisture and can drift by 3–6 g between a dry workshop and a humid garage.

How to Use the Sunlu Tare Weight

  1. Weigh the spool as it is, filament and all, on a scale that reads to 1 g (0.1 g is better but not required).
  2. Pick the matching spool type in the calculator above and type the gross weight. Remaining grams and the fraction of a full spool update instantly.
  3. Compare with your slicer's estimate for the next print. If the slicer says 180 g and you have 150 g, change spools now rather than at 3 a.m.

Worked Example

Scale reads 612 g  ·  Standard black plastic spool tare 195 g  ⇒  417 g of filament left
417 g ÷ 1000 g = 42% of a 1 kg spool
PLA 1.75 mm: 417 g ÷ (1.24 g/cm³ × 0.02405 cm³/cm) ≈ 140 m

Why Tare Weights Differ Within One Brand

  • Core material: cardboard cores run 120–170 g, plastic 180–260 g; many brands ship both depending on line and region.
  • Spool size: 750 g, 1 kg, 2 kg and 3 kg spools use different cores. The net weight column tells you which one a row refers to.
  • Revisions: brands change suppliers without announcing it. When two measurements disagree by more than 10 g, they usually come from different years.
  • Hubs and labels: plastic hubs pressed into cardboard spools, and heavy labels, add 5–15 g.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which Sunlu spool do I have?

Look at the core: cardboard is matte brown or printed, plastic is glossy. Then match the net weight on the label (1 kg is most common). If the brand has more than one row for that combination, the product line on the label decides.

My empty spool is heavier than the table says. Is the table wrong?

Probably a different batch or a bit of residual filament wound around the core. If you are confident the spool is bare and dry, submit the measurement — the median will move once enough people report the same thing.

Does humidity really change the weight?

For cardboard, yes: a few grams between seasons is common. For plastic, no. That is one reason plastic-core tares cluster more tightly in the data.

Where does this data come from?

Rows marked "meshkit" were weighed by us; "community" rows are reader submissions reviewed before publishing. Each row shows its count so you can judge how much to trust it. The dataset is CC BY 4.0 — reuse it with a link back.

Is anything tracked when I use the calculator or the form?

The calculator runs in your browser. The form sends only the fields you fill in, to a write-only queue that nobody can read from the web; there is no account and no cookie.

Reference // Measuring Properly

Unwind the last metre of filament, remove tape and clips, let a cardboard spool sit indoors for a day, then weigh on a scale zeroed with nothing on it. Report the reading to the nearest gram.
