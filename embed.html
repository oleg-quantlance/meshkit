What the Embeddable Calculator Does

One <script> tag adds a working 3D print cost calculator to any web page. Visitors type filament grams, print hours, pick a material and a markup, and get a suggested price with the material, energy and wear breakdown — computed with the same calculatePrintCost() function that powers MeshKit's full calculator and G-code estimator, so the numbers always agree. You set the defaults that fit your audience: your filament price, your currency, your printer's wattage and electricity rate, light or dark theme, and an accent colour to match your site.

The widget is 8 KB, has no dependencies, makes no network requests after loading, sets no cookies and sends nothing anywhere. It renders inside a Shadow DOM, so your page's CSS cannot break it and it cannot break your page. The only outbound link is the small "Powered by MeshKit" credit, which is required — it is how the widget stays free.

How to Embed It in 3 Steps

  1. Set your defaults in section A. Price per kg and currency are the ones that matter; everything else has sane defaults.
  2. Copy the snippet from section B. It updates live as you change the options.
  3. Paste it where you want the calculator to appear: a WordPress Custom HTML block, a Squarespace / Wix / Webflow embed element, a Shopify Custom Liquid section, or straight into your HTML. It renders right where the tag sits.

Worked Example: What the Widget Computes

Material = grams ÷ 1000 × price_per_kg
Energy = watts ÷ 1000 × hours × price_per_kWh  ·  Wear = hours × wear_rate
Unit cost = (Material + Energy + Wear) × (1 + failure% ÷ 100)
Suggested price = Unit cost × markup  ·  Profit = price − unit cost
45 g PLA at $22/kg, 3.5 h at 150 W and $0.16/kWh, 10% buffer, 3× markup → unit cost $2.34, price $7.01

Who Uses It

  • 3D-printing bloggers and YouTubers adding an interactive element to "how much does it cost to print" articles.
  • Sellers of printed goods showing a transparent "price your custom print" tool on their own storefront.
  • Print farms and makerspaces publishing their per-gram and per-hour pricing so customers can self-quote.
  • Teachers and libraries explaining print economics on a course or lab page.

Limitations

The embed covers the common case: material, energy, wear, failure buffer and a markup multiplier. Marketplace fees, target-margin pricing, time-correction factors and per-channel comparisons live in the full calculator, which the credit link opens. The widget cannot run inside platforms that strip scripts (Etsy listings, Medium posts, most email tools).

Frequently Asked Questions

Does the widget track my visitors?

No. It loads one 8 KB script from meshkit.pro and then runs entirely in the visitor's browser. No cookies, no analytics, no requests back to us. The credit link carries a UTM tag so we can see which sites send people, nothing more.

Can I remove the "Powered by MeshKit" link?

No — it is the only thing we ask in return for hosting and maintaining the widget. It is small, opens in a new tab, and uses your accent colour so it does not clash with your design.

Will it slow down my page?

The tag is async, so it never blocks rendering. 8 KB (3.5 KB over the wire) is smaller than most icons. It renders in about a millisecond once loaded.

Will updates break my page?

The URL is stable and the attributes are a contract: new options may be added, existing ones keep working. Styling is isolated in a Shadow DOM, so neither side's CSS leaks.

Can I self-host the script?

You may, but you lose automatic fixes and the numbers may drift from the live calculator over time. Hot-linking is the supported path; the file is served with CORS and a one-hour cache.

Reference // Why Shadow DOM

The widget attaches a shadow root to a div it inserts after its own script tag. Styles inside start from all: initial, so a host page's resets, grid systems and button styles cannot reach in, and the widget's styles cannot reach out.
