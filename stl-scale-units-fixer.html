What the STL Scale & Units Fixer Does

The most common "broken" STL is not broken at all — it is in the wrong unit. STL, OBJ and some 3MF exports carry no unit information, slicers assume millimetres, and a part designed in inches imports at 1/25.4 of its size (a 4-inch bracket becomes a 4 mm one). Blender exports metres by default, so a 60 mm part arrives as 0.06 units and looks empty. The second most common problem is orientation: Y-up exports from Blender, Maya and glTF pipelines land on their side. This tool fixes both, plus the long tail: exact fit-to-size, uniform or per-axis scaling, mirroring for left/right-hand parts, centring and dropping to the bed.

It opens .stl (binary or ASCII), .3mf and .obj, guesses the authoring unit from the largest extent and pre-selects it when confident, applies your choices, audits the result (watertightness, volume, orientation) and exports a binary STL in millimetres or the unit you pick. Everything runs in a Web Worker in your browser; nothing is uploaded.

How to Fix Units in 3 Steps

  1. Drop the file. The log shows the bounding box and a unit guess with its reasoning (for example "largest extent 2.36 — plausible as inches, 60 mm").
  2. Confirm or override. Set File is in to the unit the designer used; leave Write as on millimetres for any slicer. Use Fit to size when you know the real-world dimension ("this should be 100 mm tall"), which overrides guessing entirely.
  3. Orient and place. Choose Y up → Z up for Blender/Maya exports, tick Drop to bed so Z starts at 0, optionally Centre on the origin, then Export scaled .STL.

Worked Example: Blender Export

Blender default export: metres, Y up  →  a 60 mm vase arrives as 0.06 units, lying on its side
File is in: Meters (×1000)  ·  Up axis: Y up → Z up  ·  Drop to bed  ⇒  48 × 48 × 64 mm, standing, Z min = 0
Inch-authored bracket 4.000 × 1.250 × 0.375  →  ×25.4  ⇒  101.60 × 31.75 × 9.53 mm
Fit Z to 100 mm on a 64 mm model  ⇒  uniform ×1.5625; volume scales by 1.5625³ = 3.81×

Mirroring and negative per-axis factors invert the mesh; the tool flips triangle winding automatically so normals stay outward and the volume stays positive. Per-axis stretching changes proportions deliberately — use it for calibration compensation (a known 0.4% shrink in X, for instance), not for unit fixing.

When to Use This Instead of the Slicer

  • Sharing the file: fixing units in your slicer fixes it only on your machine; the downloaded STL stays wrong for everyone else.
  • Print services and quote tools read the file as-is; an inch-authored part gets quoted at 1/16,387th of its real volume.
  • Batch consistency: set the correct numbers once, then every slicer, viewer and marketplace agrees.
  • Exact target sizes: "make this 120 mm wide" is one field here; slicer scale dialogs often round or lock aspect ratios awkwardly.

Technical Limitations

The unit guess is a heuristic based on the assumption that the model is a printable part between roughly 5 mm and 500 mm; sculptures and miniatures can fool it, which is why it only pre-selects at high confidence and always shows its reasoning. 3MF unit attributes are honoured on load, so a correctly tagged 3MF is already in millimetres before your settings apply. Output is STL only; use STL to 3MF afterwards if you need a unit-tagged package.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know if my STL is in inches?

Open it here and read the bounding box. If a part you expect to be ~100 mm shows as ~4, it is in inches; if it shows as ~0.1, it is in metres. The log states the guess and why. When in doubt, use Fit to size with a dimension you know.

Does scaling change wall thickness?

Yes — uniform scaling scales everything, including walls, holes and clearances. A model scaled ×0.5 has half-thickness walls and half-size screw holes. Check minimum features after large changes.

Will mirroring break my mesh?

No. Mirroring reverses triangle winding; the tool flips it back so every face still points outward. The mesh-health cell confirms "normals outward" and a positive volume.

Why is "Drop to bed" on by default?

Most slicers place the model on the bed anyway, but viewers, quote tools and marketplace previews do not. A Z-min of 0 is the convention everything agrees on, and it costs nothing.

Is my file uploaded?

No. Parsing, transformation and export run in a Web Worker in your browser. The exported STL is generated from memory as a download.

Reference // The 25.4 Problem

STL stores bare numbers. Slicers assume millimetres. A model authored in inches imports at 1/25.4 of its size; one authored in metres (Blender's default) at 1/1000. The fixer reads the largest extent and guesses the unit, then rewrites the numbers so the slicer sees what the designer meant.
