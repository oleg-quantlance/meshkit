Five steps to a monthly P&L

  1. Settings tab. Enter your printer power draw (W), electricity rate ($ per kWh), machine wear rate ($ per hour), labour rate ($ per hour), material prices per kg, and marketplace fee percentages (Etsy, Shopify, eBay, Direct, plus a custom row).
  2. Jobs tab: log a job. One row per print (200 rows built in): date, order reference, customer, sales channel, offsite-ad toggle, item, quantity, material, total grams and hours, labour minutes, any other cost, and price charged. Five sample rows are included; overwrite or clear them.
  3. Read the row. Filament, energy, wear and labour cost, channel fees, total cost, profit and margin are calculated as soon as the price is in.
  4. Status & failures. Set the Status column to Queued, Printing, Done or Failed (and note any failure reason). Header filters let you view only Queued / Printing jobs. Failed rows keep their material/energy/wear cost and charge zero channel fees, so failures show up honestly.
  5. Monthly P&L tab. Set the yellow First month cell; 24 months roll up automatically: jobs, done vs failed count, failure rate, hours printed, grams used, revenue, costs, fees, profit and a monthly chart.

Troubleshooting

SymptomFix
#NAME? or #SPILL! in result cellsYour Excel is older than 2016, or a function is not supported. Use the Google Sheets copy (link on page 1), update Excel, or open the file in LibreOffice Calc (free).
"The cell you're trying to change is protected"Formula cells are locked so they cannot be overwritten by accident. Only the yellow input cells are meant to be edited. If you really want to change a formula: Review → Unprotect Sheet (Excel) or Data → Protect sheets and ranges (Sheets). There is no password.
A month is missing from the P&LCheck two things: (1) the yellow First month cell at the top of Monthly P&L covers your date range (24 months from that start month), and (2) the Date cell on Jobs is a real date (YYYY-MM-DD), not text.
Margin looks too high or too lowCheck the channel fee on that row and the wear rate in Settings. A wear rate of zero flatters every job; $0.10–0.50 per hour is a realistic range for hobby machines.
I ran out of rows200 job rows are pre-built. Start a fresh copy of the workbook for the new year or quarter so your file stays fast and easy to archive.
I want to price a job before logging itUse the free calculator at meshkit.pro/3d-print-cost-calculator, or the Pricing Calculator spreadsheet, then log the agreed price here.
