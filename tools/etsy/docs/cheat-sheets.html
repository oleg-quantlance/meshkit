Reading the table

Speed is an outer-wall figure for a typical direct-drive bedslinger. High-speed CoreXY machines run 2–3× these numbers with the same temperatures pushed 5–15 °C higher.

Fan % is the part-cooling fan after the first few layers. Bridges and overhangs want more; layer adhesion wants less.

Drying

Dry when you hear popping, see steam at the nozzle, or get stringing that temperature changes do not fix. A filament dryer or a food dehydrator both work; a kitchen oven overshoots and is a last resort.

Store open spools with desiccant. PETG, TPU, nylon and PC absorb moisture within days.

Density

Density converts slicer grams to spool length and cost. 1.75 mm PLA ≈ 2.98 g per metre, so a 1 kg spool is about 335 m.

*Filled filaments vary from 1.20 to 1.30 g/cm³ by fibre content; use the value on the manufacturer datasheet.

Worked example

Half-used Prusament PLA on the scale reads 712 g.

712 − 202 = 510 g left

At 2.98 g/m that is about 171 m, roughly half a spool. A 60 g print will finish with room to spare.

Change one thing at a time and reprint the same small test. Work left to right: the first fix solves most cases, the second covers the rest.

SymptomLikely causesFirst fixSecond fix
Stringing / hairs between parts Wet filament; nozzle too hot; retraction too short or too slow; slow travel moves 1Dry the filament (see sheet 1), then lower nozzle temperature 5 °C at a time with a temperature tower. 2Increase retraction distance (direct drive 0.5–2 mm, Bowden 4–7 mm) and speed to 35–45 mm/s. Warping / corners lifting Bed too cold or dirty; drafts; no enclosure for ABS/ASA/PC; sharp corners on large flat parts; part cooling too high on the first layers 1Wash the plate with dish soap and hot water (no IPA alone), raise bed temperature 5–10 °C, add a brim 5–8 mm wide. 2Block drafts or enclose the printer; turn the part-cooling fan off for the first 3–5 layers; add mouse-ear discs on corners; round sharp corners in the model.
Layer shifts Loose belt or pulley grub screw; speed or acceleration too high; nozzle catching curled edges; stepper driver overheating 1Check belt tension and tighten pulley grub screws on both motor shafts (one on the flat). Cut travel acceleration by a third. 2Enable Z-hop 0.2–0.4 mm so the nozzle clears curled overhangs. Check that the driver heatsink fan runs and that the axis moves freely by hand when powered off.
Under-extrusion (gaps, thin walls, missing lines) Partial clog; nozzle too cold for the speed; worn or slipping extruder gear; flow rate too low; filament tangle or dragging spool 1Cold-pull or swap the nozzle; raise temperature 5–10 °C or slow down 20%. Clean the extruder gear teeth. 2Calibrate E-steps / rotation distance (mark 120 mm, extrude 100 mm), then calibrate flow with a single-wall cube. Check the spool unwinds freely and the PTFE tube is seated.
Over-extrusion (blobs, rough tops, dimensions too big) Flow rate too high; wrong filament diameter in slicer (2.85 vs 1.75); nozzle too hot; E-steps too high 1Confirm filament diameter in the slicer, then lower flow/extrusion multiplier in 2% steps until a single-wall cube measures one line width. 2Lower nozzle temperature 5–10 °C; enable pressure/linear advance; check E-steps as above.
Elephant foot (first layers bulge outward) Nozzle too close on the first layer; bed too hot; weight of the part squashing soft lower layers 1Raise Z-offset by 0.02–0.05 mm; set the slicer's elephant-foot compensation to 0.1–0.2 mm. 2Lower bed temperature 5 °C after the first layer; add a 0.5 mm chamfer to the bottom edge of the model; use a raft only if nothing else works.
Poor first layer (does not stick, ripples, gaps between lines) Bed not level / mesh stale; Z-offset wrong; oily plate; first-layer speed too high; wrong surface for the material (see sheet 4) 1Wash the plate, re-run bed levelling or mesh at print temperature, then tune Z-offset live: lines should be flattened and touching, not round, not transparent. 2Slow the first layer to 15–25 mm/s, set first-layer height to 0.2–0.3 mm and width 120%, raise bed temperature 5 °C. Switch surface or add glue stick.
Clogs / jams (clicking extruder, nothing comes out) Heat creep (hotend fan weak or stopped); debris or burnt filament in the nozzle; retraction too long; PTFE tube degraded above 240 °C; wrong temperature for the material 1Heat to the material's max temperature, push filament through by hand, then do 2–3 cold pulls with nylon or cleaning filament. Replace the nozzle if it still resists. 2Check that the hotend (heat-break) fan spins at full speed from power-on; shorten retraction; trim or replace the PTFE tube and reseat it hard against the nozzle; fit an all-metal hotend for >240 °C materials.
Layers separate / cracks on tall parts Nozzle too cold; too much part cooling; drafts or no enclosure; layer height too high for the nozzle 1Raise nozzle temperature 10 °C and cut the fan to 30–50% (ABS/ASA/PC: 0–20%). 2Enclose the printer; keep layer height ≤ 75% of nozzle diameter; slow down 20% so each layer has time to bond.
Before any of the above: dry the filament, wash the plate, confirm the slicer profile matches the material on the spool. These three fix roughly half of all "my printer is broken" problems.

Clean plate, every time

Fingerprints are the number one adhesion problem. Wash PEI and glass with dish soap and hot water; dry with a paper towel. Isopropyl alcohol alone smears skin oil around instead of removing it. Never touch the print area with bare hands.

Glue stick is a release agent too

On smooth PEI, glue stick does two jobs: it stops PETG, TPU, nylon and PC from fusing to the sheet, and it adds grip on textured sheets. Water-soluble PVA stick, thin even coat, wiped with a damp cloth between prints and reapplied every 3–5 prints.

Removing stuck parts

Let the bed cool to room temperature first; most parts pop off on their own as the plate contracts. Flex spring-steel sheets. On glass, a minute in the freezer or a few drops of IPA under the edge breaks the bond. Do not pry with a sharp blade toward your hand.

Layer height vs nozzle

Keep layer height between 25% and 75% of the nozzle diameter. Below that the extrusion is squashed and inconsistent; above it layers do not bond.

NozzleMinEverydayMaxUse
0.25 mm0.060.10–0.150.18Miniatures, fine text
0.40 mm0.100.200.30Default for almost everything
0.60 mm0.150.300.45Fast functional parts
0.80 mm0.200.400.60Large, strong, quick

Line width 100–120% of nozzle diameter. First layer 0.2–0.3 mm regardless of nozzle (within the limits above).

Walls, tops, bottoms for strength

GoalWallsTop layersBottomInfill
Display / decorative23–435–10%
Everyday functional34–5415–25%
Load-bearing / brackets4–55–6530–50%
Threads, snap fits, thin posts4+5540%+

Walls add far more strength per gram than infill: going from 2 to 4 walls usually beats going from 20% to 60% infill. Add walls first, then infill.

Infill pattern

  • Gyroid: strong in every direction, no crossing lines, slower.
  • Cubic: good all-round strength, fast; the usual default.
  • Grid / lines: fast, weakest in shear; fine for display parts.
  • Concentric / lightning: top-surface support only; not structural.
  • Above about 50% infill, switch to more walls or a solid part instead.

What a print costs

material $ = grams ÷ 1000 × price per kg

Example: a 42 g part on $22/kg filament is 0.042 × 22 = $0.92 in plastic. That is rarely the whole story:

electricity $ = printer watts ÷ 1000 × hours × $/kWh
wear $ = hours × machine rate per hour
unit cost = (material + electricity + wear) × (1 + failure %)

Typical starting numbers: 120–350 W average draw, a wear rate of $0.10–0.50 per hour (printer price plus consumables ÷ expected hours), and a failure buffer of 5–15%.

Selling price is unit cost × markup (2.5–4× is common for small parts), or set a target margin and solve for price. Marketplace fees come out of the price, not the cost, so add them last:

price = (unit cost × markup + fixed fee) ÷ (1 − fee %)
Do not do this by hand. The free calculator at

Other numbers worth memorising

  • 1 kg of 1.75 mm PLA ≈ 335 m; PETG ≈ 327 m; ABS ≈ 400 m.
  • Slicer time estimates run 5–20% short on most machines; log a few real prints and apply a correction.
  • Supports typically add 10–30% material and the same in time.
  • A 0.4 mm nozzle at 0.2 mm layers extrudes about 10–12 mm³/s on a stock hotend; high-flow hotends reach 25–40 mm³/s.
  • Shrinkage to allow for in holes: PLA ≈ 0.2–0.3%, PETG ≈ 0.4–0.6%, ABS/ASA ≈ 0.8–1.0%. Add 0.1–0.2 mm to hole diameters.
