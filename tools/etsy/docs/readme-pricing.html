Five steps to a quote

  1. Settings tab. Enter your filament prices per kg, printer wattage, electricity rate, machine wear rate per hour, failure buffer, and your default markup or target margin. Pick a sales channel preset (Etsy, Shopify, direct) or type your own fee percentage and fixed fee.
  2. Quote tab. Type the grams and hours from your slicer, choose the material, add labour minutes and any post-processing or packaging. Quantity if the order is for more than one.
  3. Read the result. The quote block shows unit cost, selling price, marketplace fees, profit per piece and margin. A red flag appears if the price would be below cost.
  4. Adjust. Change markup, failure buffer or channel and watch the price move. Nothing else needs touching; the yellow cells are inputs, everything grey is calculated.
  5. Batch tab. Paste a list of parts (grams, hours, material, quantity) to price a whole order or a catalogue at once; totals and per-line margin are at the bottom.

Troubleshooting

SymptomFix
#NAME? or #SPILL! in result cellsYour Excel is older than 2016, or a function is not supported. Use the Google Sheets copy (link on page 1), update Excel, or open the file in LibreOffice Calc (free).
"The cell you're trying to change is protected"Formula cells are locked so they cannot be overwritten by accident. Only the yellow input cells are meant to be edited. If you really want to change a formula: Review → Unprotect Sheet (Excel) or Data → Protect sheets and ranges (Sheets). There is no password.
Price looks too high or too lowCheck the pricing mode in Settings. Markup multiplies unit cost (3.5× is the default). Target margin solves for a price where profit is that share of the sale. Also check the failure buffer: 10% means every tenth print is assumed to be scrap.
Fees do not match my Etsy statementThe channel preset is a typical rate; Etsy fees vary by country, payment method and offsite ads. Type your effective percentage from a recent statement into the custom fee cells.
Numbers differ from the online calculatorThey use the same formula. Compare the inputs one by one; the usual culprit is a different wear rate or a time correction percentage.
